# Registering a DMCA designated agent

**What it is.** Under US copyright law (17 U.S.C. § 512(c)), an online service that stores material at its users' direction
is protected from copyright claims about that material ("safe harbor") only if it has registered a *designated agent* with
the US Copyright Office, published the agent's contact details on its site, and acts on valid takedown notices.

**Does Women360 need it?** Today the risk is low: what people store is their own private health log, nobody publishes
anything others can see, and there are no uploads. It becomes important the moment Women360 hosts anything users can share
(community posts, comments, photos, files) or launches in the US. Registration costs **$6** and lasts **3 years**, so it is
cheap insurance. This is general guidance, not legal advice; a lawyer should confirm it once the operating entity exists.

## Before you start

You need the **legal name of the service provider** (the company or person operating Women360). This is still on the
privacy page's "Still to be confirmed" list. Register in that entity's name, not a nickname. You also need:

- Every name the service is known by: "Women360", and each address it runs at (`women-360.vercel.app`, and later your own
  domain and the app-store listings).
- A postal address for the agent. The Copyright Office accepts a post office box for the agent's address.
- The agent's name or role (a role such as "Copyright Agent" is allowed), phone number and email address. A dedicated
  address such as copyright@your-domain is better than a personal one.
- A card for the $6 fee.

## Step by step (about 15 minutes)

1. Go to the **DMCA Designated Agent Directory**: https://dmca.copyright.gov
2. **Create an account** ("Register"). The account is for whoever manages the registration; add a second contact so access
   isn't lost if one person leaves. You will confirm your email.
3. Choose **"Register a new designated agent"**.
4. **Service provider information:** the full legal name, the postal address, and under *alternate names* every name and web
   address the service uses (Women360, women-360.vercel.app, your future domain). Missing an alternate name can mean the
   protection does not cover it.
5. **Designated agent information:** name (or a role), organisation, postal address, phone, email.
6. **Review and pay** the $6 fee. You receive a registration certificate (keep the PDF with the company records).
7. **Publish the agent on the site.** Add the same agent details to the Terms page's "Copyright complaints" section (today
   it routes notices to the support address) and, if you create one, a `/copyright` page. Then tell the developer so the
   page and `docs/LAUNCH_STANDARD.md` are updated together.
8. **Put the renewal in the calendar.** The registration expires after **three years** and the directory sends reminders;
   an expired registration loses the protection. Update it (also $6) whenever any detail changes: name, address, agent, domains.

## When a notice arrives

A valid notice names the work, identifies the material and where it is, gives the sender's contact details, and includes a
good-faith statement and a statement under penalty of perjury, with a signature. Then:

1. Remove or disable access to the material promptly and tell the person who posted it.
2. If they send a valid **counter-notice**, forward it to the complainant; restore the material after 10 to 14 business days
   unless the complainant tells you they have gone to court.
3. Keep a record of every notice and action, and close the accounts of repeat infringers (the law requires a written
   repeat-infringer policy; add one to the Terms when user content exists).
