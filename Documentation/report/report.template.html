1. Abstract

Women's health tools are usually single-purpose: one app for periods, another for food, another for sleep or exercise. None of them looks across the data a person already records, most assume one fixed cycle length, and almost all are designed for a young, confident phone user. Women360 is a cloud-native SaaS platform that brings menstrual health, nutrition, activity, sleep, mental wellbeing, physical health measurements, medicines, goals and habits into one responsive web application for women from 18 to 100 and beyond.

Its primary goal is preventive care: it turns what a woman logs into early, non-diagnostic prompts, including a screening schedule based on India's national programme and WHO guidance, and observations drawn from her own baseline rather than from population averages. Its second requirement is ease of use: one-tap logging, templates and sensible defaults, and a Senior Mode that replaces the dashboard with a handful of large, plain-language tiles.

The system has three roles (Woman, Wellness Coach, Admin), each with its own web area and enforced on the server. It is a React 18 + TypeScript single-page application hosted on Vercel, talking over HTTPS to a Node.js/Express REST API (about 110 endpoints) hosted on Render, which stores data in a managed PostgreSQL database on Supabase through the Prisma ORM. Source control, automated checks and deployment are driven from GitHub. The application is live at the URL above.

2. Introduction

Women360 started as the BCSE408L Cloud Computing project and has been built as a real product rather than a coursework demonstration. The Software Requirements Specification (SRS) fixes the scope: secure registration and login, role-based access, profile management, health and wellness tracking with full CRUD, dashboards and reports, notifications, a cloud database, REST APIs, and public deployment on a cloud platform. The project must demonstrate cloud concepts, and is not considered complete if it runs only on localhost.

The product is deliberately a wellness, tracking and decision-support application. It does not diagnose, prescribe, or present a screening schedule as a clinical instruction (SRS §12).

3. Problem Statement

  • Fragmentation. A woman's cycle, sleep, food, movement and mood affect one another, but each is tracked in a separate place, so nobody can ask "why is my period late?" or "why has my mood been low?" against her own data.
  • False precision. Fixed assumptions (a 28-day cycle, ovulation on day 14) are wrong for many people and unsafe when read as a contraception signal, especially with irregular cycles, thyroid conditions, PCOS or perimenopause.
  • Tracking fatigue. Retyping the same meal or activity every day is the most common reason people stop. Shame-based feedback (red numbers, lost streaks) can also encourage unhealthy behaviour.
  • Prevention is invisible. Most tools record what has happened; few remind a woman which check-ups are due at her age, or in her country.
  • Age and accessibility. Interfaces are designed for the young. A woman in her seventies needs a different structure, not just a bigger font.
  • Privacy. Health data is sensitive; users should be able to see, export and erase it, and share it only deliberately.

4. Existing System

Existing consumer apps fall into separate categories: cycle trackers, calorie counters, sleep trackers, fitness apps, meditation apps and medication reminders. Each treats its own metric in isolation and has a recognised weakness:

ApproachLimitation addressed by Women360
Cycle trackersSingle-date predictions from a fixed formula; weak for irregular cycles and perimenopause; fertile-window estimates easily misread as contraception.
Calorie / nutrition appsEvery entry needs a full form; red/green feedback and streaks; no link to how the person feels or sleeps.
Sleep appsA single score can make people anxious about a number and contradict how they actually feel.
Medication remindersReminders without any view of adherence over time or its link to wellbeing.
Generic health dashboardsNot designed for older users; no preventive-care schedule; limited control over data export or deletion.

5. Proposed System

Women360 is a single cloud-hosted application with a stateless REST API and a managed database. Its main advantages:

  • One place for the whole picture, with a cross-domain "explain this" engine that compares recent sleep, food, activity, hydration, stress and weight against that person's own baseline and reports ranked, disclaimed observations. A late period is never simply labelled "maybe pregnant", and low mood is never simply "PMS".
  • Honest cycle predictions: median and spread over her own cycles, shown as a range with a confidence level, with a persistent "not a birth control method" notice.
  • Preventive screening: a per-age schedule (blood pressure, blood sugar, oral, breast and cervical screening) with named sources, "next due" dates and one-tap recording.
  • Low-effort logging: one-tap quick logs, "log again" meal chips, one-tap habit ticks, forms that never pre-fill an unentered value.
  • Senior Mode: a structural change to navigation and information architecture, not a zoom.
  • Privacy by design: every record belongs to one user, data can be exported and the account erased, and coach access is role-limited.
  • Cloud-native delivery: free-tier managed services, git-driven deployment, database migrations applied automatically on deploy, and a CI pipeline.

8. Non-Functional Requirements

RequirementHow it is addressed
Security (NFR-01)Passwords hashed with Argon2id. Short-lived access tokens (15 minutes) and refresh tokens (30 days). Helmet security headers, CORS allow-list, rate limiting (20 requests per 15 minutes on the authentication endpoints, 120 per minute on the API). Every request body validated with zod. Sign-in lockout (5 per account, 40 per IP, 15 minutes) stored in the database so it survives restarts. Optional TOTP two-factor with AES-256-GCM-encrypted secrets and backup codes. Admin accounts cannot be created through the API. The shared demonstration logins published for evaluators are low-power by design: the demonstration administrator is read-only and sees only demonstration accounts, and none of them can change a password, delete the account, use two-factor or sign out everywhere. Security-relevant events are written to an audit log without emails or secrets. The web app sends a Content-Security-Policy and related headers. CI runs a dependency audit.
Privacy (NFR-02)Each record carries its owner; every service checks ownership before reading or changing it (tested for cross-user access). A person can download everything held about her account and permanently erase it. Coaches see a limited summary and never the health profile or medicines.
Scalability (NFR-03)Stateless API instances behind a platform load balancer; managed PostgreSQL with indexes on the per-user query paths; static front end served from a CDN. Moving to a paid tier or more instances needs no code change.
Availability (NFR-04)Managed platforms with a health-check endpoint. On the free tier the API sleeps when idle; the first request after a pause took about 22 seconds in a test on 22 October 2026, and later requests were fast.
Performance (NFR-05)Route-level code splitting (heavy pages such as Reports are loaded only when visited); data fetched per page; the landing page loads no WebGL.
Reliability (NFR-06)Schema changes ship as committed migrations applied on deploy; CI fails if the schema and the migrations disagree. Errors return a uniform envelope and failed saves show an error rather than silently dropping data.
Usability (NFR-07)One-tap logging, templates, progressive disclosure, plain language, no streak guilt, honest empty states, and Senior Mode.
Responsiveness (NFR-08)Layouts checked at phone and desktop widths with no horizontal scrolling; a bottom navigation bar on phones.
Maintainability (NFR-09)Strict TypeScript on both sides, ESLint, one folder per domain on the server and a service layer on the client, 322 automated tests, and a written engineering standard in the repository.
Portability (NFR-10)All configuration through environment variables; a Render blueprint (render.yaml) and a step-by-step DEPLOYMENT.md reproduce the deployment from the repository.

9. System Architecture

Request flow. Logging a meal, for example: a page component calls nutritionService; apiClient.request() adds the Bearer token and sends HTTPS to /api/v1/nutrition/meals; Express runs the middleware chain; the zod schema rejects malformed input; nutrition.service.ts applies the business rules and writes through Prisma; the API answers with { ok, data } or { ok: false, error } and the page updates.

Boundaries. No component calls fetch directly. A shared mappers.ts is the single place that translates the server's UPPER_SNAKE_CASE enums and date formats into the client's vocabulary. On the server, routes only wire HTTP to handlers, services hold the logic, and Prisma is the only code that touches SQL.

Integrity. Required fields and ranges are validated by zod before any write; relations use foreign keys with cascade delete; unique constraints stop duplicate daily rows (one sleep entry, one habit log, one steps log per day). 13. User Interface Design

The interface uses a deep maroon palette on warm neutrals with a serif display face (Fraunces) and Inter for text, defined once as design tokens with a real dark mode. UI primitives (buttons, cards, modals, tabs, toasts, empty, loading and error states) are shared across every page. Figures 3 to 24 are screenshots of the running application; the data shown is synthetic.

Responsive and accessible by design

  • Desktop shows a sidebar; phones show a bottom bar; Senior Mode swaps the structure completely.
  • No horizontal scrolling at phone (375 to 390 px) and desktop widths (checked while building each page).
  • Semantic HTML, labelled inputs, visible focus, keyboard-operable controls and screen-reader announcements for status messages.
  • Animations respect the reduced-motion setting.
  • Light and dark themes follow the device unless the person chooses one.

Administrator and coach areas

Administrators and coaches do not use the woman's sidebar or Senior Mode. Each gets a plain header and one working page, reached automatically when signing in. API conventions

All endpoints live under /api/v1. Responses use one envelope: { "ok": true, "data": ... } or { "ok": false, "error": { "code", "message" } }. Validation failures return 422, missing or invalid tokens 401, ownership or role violations 403, unknown records 404, and rate-limited requests 429.

Cloud-related components

  • Configuration: database URL, JWT secrets, CORS origins and the optional two-factor key are environment variables on Render; the front end gets only the public API base URL from Vercel. No secret is in the repository.
  • Migrations on deploy: the Render build runs prisma migrate deploy, so a push that changes the schema also updates the cloud database.
  • Demonstration accounts: the Render build runs npm run seed:demo, which (only when SEED_DEMO_ACCOUNTS=true) creates the three shared logins and refreshes the demonstration woman's synthetic data. A failure there never blocks a deploy.
  • Waking the free-tier API: the landing and sign-in pages call the health endpoint as they load, so the server wakes while the person reads instead of at sign-in.
  • Health check: GET /health is used by Render and by the deployment test.
  • Database-backed lockout: sign-in attempt counters live in PostgreSQL because the free-tier API sleeps and an in-memory counter would reset.
  • Security headers: the Content-Security-Policy restricts scripts to the site itself and connections to the site and the API.

16. Deployment

  1. Database. Create a Supabase project and copy its PostgreSQL connection string.
  2. API on Render. Create a Blueprint from the repository. Render reads render.yaml (root directory server, Singapore region, free plan, health check /health). Set DATABASE_URL, JWT_ACCESS_SECRET, JWT_REFRESH_SECRET and CORS_ORIGINS (the blueprint also sets SEED_DEMO_ACCOUNTS). The build installs dependencies, generates the Prisma client, compiles TypeScript, runs prisma migrate deploy and then creates the demonstration logins.
  3. Front end on Vercel. Import the repository and set VITE_API_BASE_URL to the API's /api/v1 URL. vercel.json adds the SPA rewrite and security headers.
  4. Connect. Add the Vercel URL to CORS_ORIGINS on Render so the browser may call the API.
  5. Verify. Open /health, register an account on the live site, and log a record.
  6. Updates. A push to main deploys both services; schema changes migrate the database on the same deploy.

The step-by-step guide is DEPLOYMENT.md in the repository.

Observations

  • The layered design let the front end be built against the real API from the start and let new modules (habits, screening, quick log) be added as additive migrations without touching existing data.
  • Writing the guidance for screening as one reviewed file with named sources, and showing its status on screen, keeps medical claims out of the interface code.
  • The free tier's cold start (about 22 s) is the main user-visible limit. It is a hosting-plan trade-off, not a design limit.
  • Publishing logins in a report is a real risk, so the shared demonstration accounts are deliberately limited: the demonstration administrator is read-only and sees only demonstration accounts, and no demonstration account can change a password, delete itself or use two-factor.

Known limitations

  • Steps are entered by hand. Automatic sync from a watch needs a native integration (Android Health Connect, Apple HealthKit) and a real device to test, and is not built.
  • Reminders appear in the app when it is opened; there is no push or email delivery.
  • The screening schedule follows named India and WHO sources but has not been reviewed by a clinician, and says so on screen.
  • Not yet built: social-connection indicators (FR-11), a global search (FR-17), and a browser end-to-end test suite.
  • Hosting: the free API tier sleeps when idle; the landing and sign-in pages wake it in the background, but a first request after a long pause can still take 20 to 30 seconds.

18. Future Enhancements

  • Step counts from a smartwatch: read steps from Android Health Connect (which Wear OS, Samsung and Fitbit watches sync into) through the Android app, and from Apple Health in an iOS app.
  • Richer coach and administrator tools: coach messaging, per-field sharing choices, and bulk administration.
  • Clinical review of the screening schedule and educational content, then localisation by country (SRS §16).

  • A paid hosting tier (no cold start) and automated backups with a tested restore.

Test userThe woman's application at /app with a month of synthetic data (cycle, nutrition, activity, sleep, wellbeing, goals, habits, medicines, check-ups). Records can be added, edited and deleted freely. Settings, Privacy & sharing shows the demonstration coach already has access.
Wellness coachThe coach area at /coach: the demonstration woman's shared summary and notes. Notes can be added.
AdminThe administrator area at /admin: overview, users, audit log, feedback and Learn content. This is a read-only demonstration administrator that sees only the three demonstration accounts, so nothing can be changed or deleted.

For safety the demonstration accounts cannot change their password, delete themselves, turn on two-factor or sign out everywhere, so the logins stay valid for every evaluator. Full administrator accounts exist only for the operator and are created from the command line.
